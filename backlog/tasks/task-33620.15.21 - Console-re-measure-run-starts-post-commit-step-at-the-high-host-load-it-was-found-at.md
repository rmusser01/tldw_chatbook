## Implementation Notes

<!-- SECTION:NOTES:BEGIN -->
**Result.** At host load1 99-127, no post-commit piece has a median above 10 ms, so AC#2 files no follow-up for a piece. The largest piece medians are the trace request at 2.8-3.3 ms wall, the workspace projection at 2.2-3.1 and the acceptance projection at 2.2-2.3. The identity publication that TASK-33620.15.8 was filed about takes 0.17-0.25 ms.

The step as a whole:
- At high load its median is 16.6 ms wall (13.1 ms CPU) on first sends and 14.0 ms (10.2) on later sends.
- At low load it is 8.2 and 6.2 ms.
- Its tail is long: 8 of 48 steps took 50 ms or more of wall time (maximum 235 ms) on only 6-18 ms of CPU.

Run start's longest callback at high load is usually not the post-commit step. It is another run-task callback, most often the MCP catalog fingerprint's recovery-activation check, filed as TASK-33620.15.38. No production code or test changed: this task only measures.

**Set-up.** Both arms are origin/dev 7536ac3b87; the only variable is host load. Each launch used a fresh isolated profile and a null keyring and made 4 sends to the real Anthropic claude-haiku-4-5 through the review proxy, at 160x45. Sends started 25 s after the first render (45 s at high load). Three rounds:
- r1: 2 low-load launches, then 4 high-load, then 2 low-load.
- r2: 4 high-load launches, then 2 low-load.
- r3: 4 high-load launches.

That gives 48 sends at load1 99.2-127.0 and 24 at 2.6-9.4. All 72 Enters reached the provider and got reply headers, counted by the probe's POST records before any timing was read. The app logs have no ERROR lines (4 WARNING lines per launch, all the provider's temperature/top_p notice). One capture did not show the typed prompt before Enter (m21chi1, first send), but that send still reached the provider and is counted.

**Load.** `kit/m21load.py` ran 88 busy-loop `python -I -S` children. Each child had its own deadline and a SIGALRM backstop, and the generator was capped at 1500 s. The round script stopped it after each high-load block, and a check afterwards found no child left running. load1 went from about 4 to 75 in 70-75 s, and fell back under 12 in 145-175 s after the stop.

The original rounds' load came from other sessions' work. A pure busy loop drops in priority under macOS's timeshare scheduler, so it may delay the app less than mixed work does. Even so, callbacks with 2-4 ms of CPU ran 60-300 ms wall at this load.

**Probe.** The probe ran from PYTHONPATH only: `kit/pf2probe_impl.py` with `sitecustomize.py`, never importing tldw_chatbook outside the app. It recorded:
- each callback's wall, CPU and GC time;
- 4 ms main-thread samples, dropping frames before formatting;
- exact inclusive wall and CPU timers per callback on about 40 named methods. The post-commit step was identified as the callback that ran `publish_durable_turn_identity`.

The probe grew between rounds. r1's high-load launches had only the post-commit timers. r1's last two low-load launches and r2 added the run-start site timers. r2's last high-load launch and r3 added the prompt-history file job timers. The prompt-history medians below therefore cover only the 20 high-load and 8 low-load sends that had those timers.

The prompt-history job has two levels. On a launch's first two sends it takes 3.0-4.7 ms at high load (2.0-3.1 at low load), and one second send took 30.8 ms. On the third and fourth sends it takes 0.2-0.6 ms at both loads. The later-send column pools second sends with third and fourth ones, so its median understates the second send.

In 7 of the 8 high-load steps of 50 ms or more, one piece held most of the wall time while the thread waited to run again:
- The trace request, 4 times.
  - In three of them the wait was in transaction entry: 43.8, 142.8 and 167.2 ms across the step's two entries, on 1.4-2.1 ms of CPU. It was not SQLite. `TransactionContextManager.__enter__` first enters storage admission (`_core_operation` > `_repository_operation` > `acquire_storage` > `_execution_selection_for`), and only then does `_enter_transaction` issue BEGIN IMMEDIATE. Of the samples in those three steps, 6 of 7, 23 of 25 and 29 of 40 sit in that admission's `Path.resolve` under the trace request's transaction entry, on `realpath`'s stat calls. None is on the BEGIN.
  - In the fourth (a 69 ms step, 59 ms of it in the trace request), 9 of the step's 11 samples were on `uuid4`, which reads `os.urandom`, while minting a trace id.
  - The 235 ms step, one of the three, also spent 58 ms in `ensure_policy`'s write-intent claim, waiting on an SQL execute (0.1 ms of CPU).
- The workspace projection, once: 210 ms wall, 3.5 ms CPU.
- The acceptance projection, once: 132 ms wall, 1.6 ms CPU.
- The prompt-history executor submit, once: 145 ms, recorded before that piece had a timer. Its samples sat on `SimpleQueue.put`, where the UI loop wakes the worker thread.

The eighth (83 ms, m21chi2's second send) had no majority piece. It split across the prompt-history file job (30.8 ms), the identity publication (23.0 ms) and the trace request (19.3 ms). The identity publication's time was its Library-policy read: 22.7 ms on 0.3 ms of CPU. That is the only one of the 48 high-load sends where the identity publication, TASK-33620.15.8's suspect, took more than 1.1 ms.

Pieces that release the GIL (SQLite, stat calls, waking a thread) collect these waits. Of the 184 samples taken inside the step at high load, 89 were in storage admission's `Path.resolve`: 70 of them inside a ChaChaNotes transaction's entry, and none in the BEGIN itself. 97 were in the trace request and 7 in the identity publication. The ranking that TASK-33620.15.8 was filed from (identity publication 34 samples, trace request and workspace projection 13 each) does not reproduce under exact timers.

No piece's median crosses 10 ms, so AC#2 files nothing. The review of this task asked for the shared cause behind 3 of the tails to be filed anyway: storage admission resolves paths with stat calls on the UI loop when a ChaChaNotes transaction is entered. That is TASK-33620.15.47.
