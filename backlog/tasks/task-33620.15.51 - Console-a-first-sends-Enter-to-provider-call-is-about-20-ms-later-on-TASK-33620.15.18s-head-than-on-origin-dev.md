## Description

<!-- SECTION:DESCRIPTION:BEGIN -->
Found in review of TASK-33620.15.18. Live, paired and interleaved, real Anthropic claude-haiku-4-5, 160x45, established isolated profiles, one first send per launch typed 3 s after the pre-import pass ended and sent 1.5 s later: Enter to provider POST was a paired median +21 ms later on that task's head f1b164cc2f than on origin/dev 7536ac3b87 in two independent rounds (8 pairs, slower in 6; 16 pairs on fresh profiles, slower in 10, diffs -110 to +80 ms). Both arms imported the same 3 modules on the UI thread between Enter and the POST and the same modules on other threads, and their GC time there was equal. UI-loop callback CPU in that span was equal in the first round (383 / 382 ms) and a paired median +11.6 ms higher on the head in the second (373 / 365 ms), spread over screen updates and message pumps rather than one callback. The head's changes that persist after the pass: boot freezes count instead of walking the frozen set, the warm-up freezes after each step (54 freezes instead of 1), and its steps are split per third-party package. Against the same code without the warm-up, the head was -14 ms (8 pairs) and imported 3 modules on the UI thread instead of 64. If origin/dev is really about 21 ms ahead of the head, the head keeps the warm-up's import gain but gives back part of its time gain. No mechanism has been found.

## Implementation Notes

<!-- SECTION:NOTES:BEGIN -->
Result: on current origin/dev a first send reaches the provider no later, within this design's noise, than it does with PR #3336's runtime changes reverted, so AC #2's condition is not met and no code changed. The design cannot resolve a 20 ms effect in either direction. The +21 ms this task was filed for was measured on TASK-33620.15.18's reviewed head f1b164cc2f, which froze the heap after each of 54 warm-up steps. #3336 merged with one more commit, 5a70ec1977, which replaced those freezes with one idle-maintenance request at the pass's end. So the ACs were re-scoped to the merged code (dated in each), and f1b164cc2f itself was not re-measured. On the way the probe found two other things, filed as TASK-33620.15.56 and TASK-33620.15.57.

Method. Every send was live: real Anthropic claude-haiku-4-5 through the review proxy, 160x45, isolated profiles (four, each seeded by three real launches with the catalog check declined), and one first send per launch. The prompt was typed 3.0 s after the first-send warm-up returned and sent 1.5 s later. All 96 measured sends got HTTP 200 and showed the reply. The probe is a PYTHONPATH-only sitecustomize derived from tools/pf2probe. It records:
- the warm-up's start and end (the trigger, written once);
- every UI-loop callback's thread CPU, and every callback in the send window by kind;
- GC passes with CPU, imports by thread, and collector calls (gc.freeze, get_freeze_count, collect, get_objects);
- boot freezes and the refreezer's request, fire and maintenance;
- the send stages and the requests POST to /anthropic/v1/messages.

Latency runs from the Enter's injection to that POST. One launch's wall clock stepped 200 ms against the app's clock, so it was re-anchored on its typed burst (lesson added).

For about two hours that afternoon, other agents ran 88-process load generators, and their test runs held host idle CPU at 0-60%. So pairs started only with no generator running and idle CPU at the gate, and were re-run if a generator appeared at Enter or 3 s after. In the two rounds, load1 at Enter was 5.9-18.0. The power log shows no sleep during the rounds.

Arms, all on origin/dev 29592ecc9b:
- **D:** dev as is.
- **R:** dev with #3336's runtime changes reverted by hand:
  - the third-party steps and textual_serve dropped, and the canvas modules back in their old single order;
  - the input-quiet wait removed;
  - the closing `freeze_long_lived_heap("first_send_preimport")` instead of the idle request, and the busy predicate without the navigation and custody checks;
  - boot freezes walking the frozen set before and after.

  Dev's later additions to the module list were kept.
- **Leave-one-out arms:** D with only the walking boot freeze restored (walk), only the old steps (steps), or only the old completion (completion). This is the merged-code form of "the commit before each of its three runtime changes": on dev the per-step freezes no longer exist, and the completion change replaced them. The input-quiet wait got no arm, because with no input during the pass it returns at once.

AC #1, dev against the revert (evidence/bd5/P51/r1, r2):
- **Rounds:** two rounds of 8 ABBA pairs, with D on profile A and R on B, then swapped.
  - Round 1: paired median -75 ms, D faster in 7 of 8.
  - Round 2: -25 ms, D faster in 4 of 8. Two R launches took 2.97 s and 3.13 s, both on a loaded host (below).
- **All 16 pairs:** Enter-to-POST medians 1,550 ms (D) and 1,634 ms (R). Paired median -60 ms, bootstrap 95% CI -176 to +18 ms, D slower in 5 of 16 (sign test p = 0.21). UI-loop callback CPU in the span had a paired median of -16 ms, and main-thread GC -1.3 ms.
- **Sensitivity to host load and profile:** round 2's gate sampled idle CPU (at least 15%) only before each pair, and other agents' test runs then loaded the host inside two of its pairs. s5's R launch sent at 1.1% idle, and both s6 launches at 11-13%. Those are round 2's two largest differences (-1,415 and -737 ms).
  - Without them, the 14 other pairs give a paired median of -52 ms (CI -103 to +26), D slower in 5 of 14.
  - Round 2's six other pairs had D a median 67 ms slower, where round 1's eight had it 75 ms faster. D ran on profile A in round 1 and on B in round 2, so this reads as a profile effect of about 70 ms rather than an arm effect.
  - Profile-balanced (the mean of the two rounds' medians, round 2 without s5 and s6): -4 ms (CI -119 to +45).
  - So dev is not later than the revert within noise, and the +21 ms is inside every interval here: this design cannot resolve an effect that small either way.
- **Where the time went:** the span runs about 0.3 s to the durable commit, about 1.15 s from there to the trace reservation (on a worker), then about 0.15 s to the POST. Round 1's lead built up in the middle segment. That middle segment is the saved-to-trace span TASK-34601 is attributing.
- **Imports:** at Enter both arms had the same modules loaded (except app_idle_warmup), and in all 32 launches both imported the same 14 tldw modules on the UI thread before the POST (TASK-33620.15.56).

AC #1, attribution (evidence/bd5/P51/p3):
- **Design:** 16 blocks, each launching the four arms in a Williams order. Profiles rotated per quarter, so each arm met each profile in 4 blocks.
  - A first attempt (p2) gave D only the two older profiles and was discarded after 4 blocks: the two newest profiles ran 75-95 ms faster.
  - Blocks c8 and c9 (the host's idle CPU fell to 0-35% inside them), c10 (killed mid-launch) and c13 (its D launch ran 28 minutes before the rest) were re-run.
- **Arm effects against D,** adjusted for block and profile:

  | Arm | All 64 launches | 49 launches with ≥ 30% idle CPU before the send |
  |---|---|---|
  | walk | +185 ms (95% CI +6 to +368) | +192 ms (+37 to +379) |
  | steps | -17 ms (-186 to +138) | +30 ms (-109 to +146) |
  | completion | +105 ms (-97 to +310) | -2 ms (-103 to +126) |

  So no reverted change makes the first send detectably earlier than dev. The steps arm's point estimate is -17 ms, well inside its interval.
- **Which changes can act during the send:** none of the walk and step changes' code runs in the send window. No arm ran a boot freeze, a frozen-set walk, a freeze or idle maintenance between Enter and the POST, and every arm had the same modules at Enter. After the pass, only the completion differs.
- **Why the walk arm lags:** mostly the launches that skipped the pass-end re-freeze (below). Without them (54 launches in 16 blocks) its effect is +27 ms (-193 to +226). Also leaving out launches under 30% idle CPU (42 launches in 15 blocks), it is +93 ms (-92 to +212).

AC #2: not triggered. Dev is not slower than the revert, and no change was applied, so nothing TASK-33620.15.18 removed comes back: the frozen-set walks on boot freezes, the native_authority-sized step, and the UI-loop generation-2 pass during a click.

Found on the way, both measured and filed (neither is the +21 ms):
- **TASK-33620.15.56:** the 14 UI-thread imports above. TASK-33620.15.18 counted 3 such imports in October.
- **TASK-33620.15.57:** in some launches the warm-up's modules stay unfrozen through the first send.
  - How: the pass-end idle request's timer fires 2.0 s after the warm-up and usually re-freezes. Because runtime re-freezes count as growth, it sometimes finds a compaction due instead and defers, and the first send then drops the request. The first send runs with the warm-up's modules unfrozen: a median 69 ms of main-thread GC in the span, against 45 ms. The first reply's maintenance then compacts for 241-298 ms on the UI loop.
  - How often: dev 4 of 36 launches, walk 7 of 20, steps 3 of 19, but 0 of 36 with the old closing boot freeze.
  - ADR-198's 2026-11-01 amendment now carries a dated note with these numbers.
- **Not a regression:** the refreezer's own UI-loop walks after the pass (TASK-33620.15.43) were a median 62.6 ms per launch on D and 60.5 ms on R.
