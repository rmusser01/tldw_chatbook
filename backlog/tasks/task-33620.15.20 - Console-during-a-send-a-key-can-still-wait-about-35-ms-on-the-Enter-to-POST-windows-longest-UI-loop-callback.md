## Description

<!-- SECTION:DESCRIPTION:BEGIN -->
Found by the independent review of TASK-33620.15.8 (2026-10-23). That task halved run start's own longest UI-loop callback (first sends 23.8 -> 10.6 ms, later sends 16.8 -> 8.6 ms, paired live against origin/dev 5b5920b8e2, Anthropic haiku, load 4-23). The longest callback anywhere between Enter and the provider POST did not move: first sends 38.2 -> 34.4 ms (paired -0.6 ms, 7 of 11 better), later sends 33.5 -> 35.0 ms (paired +0.9 ms, 11 of 28). So a key pressed during a send can still wait about 35 ms on some callback that is not run start's, and which callback that is has not been identified. Separately, that task's probe-free Enter-to-POST rounds (28 launches per arm) had paired ranges of -644..+493 ms on first sends and -1053..+373 ms on later ones: the medians showed no regression, but a small one could not be ruled out.

## Implementation Notes

<!-- SECTION:NOTES:BEGIN -->
Partial. The largest attributable cause is fixed: the first poll tick after a send's acceptance no longer restyles the Conversations tray's throwaway diff composes. AC#1 is met. AC#2 is not: the longest callback is now a whole-screen reflow and paint (filed: TASK-33620.15.26 for its avoidable part). AC#3 could not be shown at today's host load.

**Measured first (AC#1).** Live through `launch.sh` (isolated profile, null keyring), Anthropic claude-haiku-4-5 through the review proxy and a byte relay, 160x45. The pf2 probe kit was extended with exact per-callback timers (Textual screen update, reflow and paint phases), a record of each reflow's resized and moved widgets, and every `App.update_styles` call with its app-frame caller. origin/dev 3be493413a, host load 5-12, 13 probed sends into one growing chat (4 first after launch, 9 later):
- First sends: the window's longest callback had a median of 55 ms (33 ms CPU). In 4 of 4 it was the first 0.2 s poll tick after acceptance, 260-355 ms after Enter. In that tick `ConsoleWorkspaceContextTray.sync_state` takes TASK-36012's in-place patch for the "Running" marker, which composes the tray twice off the DOM to diff it. Each class set during those composes restyles the new node at once (the compose helpers' `add_class`/`remove_class`, and two `set_class` calls in every `Button` constructor): 64-69 `App.update_styles` calls, 21-34 ms of the tick.
- Later sends: median 47.8 ms (39.8 ms CPU). That poll tick was longest in 5 of 9 sends by wall time (8 of 9 by CPU). A whole-screen reflow and paint was longest in 3, and an asyncio self-pipe wake (GIL wait) in 1.
- Both medians are above the 35 ms this task was filed at. TASK-36012 (#3257), which added those composes, merged after the 2026-10-26 re-profile on 0d7e418db1.

**Fix.** `compose_delta.detached_styles_skipped(app)`. While the tray builds its two diff composes, `App.update_styles` skips nodes that have no parent. The app and every mounted node are restyled as before, and the previous binding is restored on every exit. Neither tree is ever mounted, and Textual styles a widget again when it mounts (`App._register`), so nothing visible changes. Files: `Widgets/Console/compose_delta.py`, `Widgets/Console/console_workspace_context.py`.

**Tests.** `Tests/UI/test_console_tray_patch_styles.py` (new; added at the UI PR gate census tail; after the rebase onto 416feb50ee the floor is dev's 964 + 1 = 965):
- The patch's two composes style no parentless node, and the live row's class write is still styled (positive control).
- The helper styles live nodes, skips detached ones, and restores the binding on a raising exit.
- On origin/dev 3be493413a both fail: 72 throwaway nodes styled (`Button`, `ConsoleBrowserSearchInput`), and the helper does not exist.
- Three mutations were each caught: a helper that skips nothing, an inverted predicate, and a missing restore.
- TASK-36012's A/B (`test_running_marker_patch_matches_a_recompose`: geometry, paint, classes and tooltips against a rebuild) stays green.

Headless CPU for a patched running-marker sync (app stylesheets, `process_time` inside `sync_state`, median of 9 alternating syncs, two interleaved runs per arm): 92 and 92 ms on dev against 13 and 10 ms on the branch at 12 rows; 290 and 279 ms against 26 and 24 ms at 40 rows.
