## Description

<!-- SECTION:DESCRIPTION:BEGIN -->
Found by TASK-33620.15.21's live rounds (2026-10-30, origin/dev 7536ac3b87, Anthropic claude-haiku-4-5 via the review proxy, 160x45, a fresh isolated profile per launch). 48 sends ran at host load1 99-127 from a bounded busy-loop generator, and 24 at load1 3-9.

With the probe on, Enter to the provider POST had a median of 8.5 s on first sends and 6.3 s on later sends at high load, against 1.4 s and 1.07 s at low load. Run start's longest UI-loop callback at high load had a median of about 130 ms, so no single hold explains a gap of 5-7 s.

What the probe did record: the UI-loop callbacks of 2 ms or more inside the window add up to a median of 4.0 s of wall time but only 0.86 s of CPU on first sends (124 callbacks), and 2.9 s wall and 0.63 s CPU on later sends (87 callbacks). That is about 47% of the window, against about 24% at low load. The rest of the window, about 4.5 s on first sends, is time the loop spent in shorter callbacks or idle, presumably waiting on worker threads, I/O or the network. Nothing has measured it.

Unknowns to settle before any fix:
- How much of the gap is the probe's own cost under load (it samples the main thread every 4 ms and wraps about 40 methods).
- How much is the shape of the load. A pure busy loop drops in priority under macOS's timeshare scheduler, so mixed real work may delay the app differently.
- Which waits fill the idle part of the window: worker-thread queueing, source admissions, provider client set-up, or something else.

Evidence: tools/evidence/bd5/M21/ (rounds r1-r3, all_sends.json, analysis_all.txt).
