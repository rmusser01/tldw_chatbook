## Description

<!-- SECTION:DESCRIPTION:BEGIN -->
From TASK-33621.80's review. A work chain's wall clock starts at its first accepted wake. When a later wake of the same chain is paused at the Ask compaction trigger (code compaction_ask), the clock keeps running while the chain waits for the user. A /compact long after the pause can then find the chain past its wall-clock budget: the retry is refused (wall_budget) and the fleet section changes from 'Paused: context limit' to 'Paused: time limit'. A chain's first wake is unaffected, because its clock has not started. Found in review by reading; not reproduced.

**Owner decision, 2026-11-12, re-confirmed 2026-11-19:** "PAUSE THE CLOCK — time waiting on a compaction_ask hold does not count against the automatic-work wall clock. The chain's time limit measures only time the work actually runs; a /compact after a long wait resumes normally." Recorded as an amendment to ADR-134. Other holds the user must lift (tool approval waits, the auto-off pause) are unchanged; TASK-33621.154 asks the owner about them.

## Implementation Plan

<!-- SECTION:PLAN:BEGIN -->
Owner decision (2026-11-12, re-confirmed 2026-11-19): PAUSE THE CLOCK. Time a chain spends held at the Ask compaction trigger (compaction_ask) does not count against its automatic-work wall clock.
1. Read the ledger (DB/automatic_work.py): the chain clock is started_at/deadline_at (wall, durable) plus started_monotonic/clock_owner_id (this process). Status leaves 'paused' only in _check_admission, pause(), the refusal path and recovery.
2. No schema change: last_observed_at already is a durable wall stamp. pause(compaction_ask) folds elapsed time (max of wall and monotonic, as the admission check counts it) into the wall anchors and stamps the hold's start. The hold ends at the chain's next admission, or when another pause code replaces it: the wall anchors move forward by the held wall time and the monotonic anchor is re-based in this process, so neither a sleep nor a clock step during the hold adds or removes running time. 3. The refusal path keeps a hold's end (started_at/deadline_at join the clock observations it restores) so a refused first retry neither loses the hold nor counts it twice; an unstarted clock stays unstarted.

## Implementation Notes

<!-- SECTION:NOTES:BEGIN -->
Time a chain waits at the Ask compaction hold (code compaction_ask) no longer counts against its automatic-work wall clock, so a /compact long after the pause resumes the wake instead of refusing it as wall_budget ("Paused: time limit"). AC#1: the owner's decision (2026-11-12, re-confirmed 2026-11-19) is in the description and in a new ADR-134 amendment. AC#2: the clock leaves the hold out. AC#3: ledger and real-controller tests pin it.

**Design (DB/automatic_work.py only; no schema change, no migration).**
- `_CLOCK_HOLD_REASONS = {"compaction_ask"}`. Pausing a chain with it stamps the hold's start in `last_observed_at`, a durable wall reading that nothing else moves while the chain is held. It also folds the elapsed time so far (the larger of the wall and the in-process monotonic reading, as admission counts it) into `started_at`/`deadline_at`. A second compaction_ask pause while held changes nothing.
- The hold ends at the chain's next admission (`_check_admission`, before the wall check) or when another pause code replaces it (`pause()` and the refusal path). Then `started_at` and `deadline_at` move forward by the held wall time, the monotonic anchor is re-based in the current process (`clock_owner_id`), and the stamp moves to that moment, so two ends never exclude the same interval. The running time before and after the hold is the same whatever either clock did during it: a sleep, a forward step, an app restart. A backward step still refuses as clock_reversed.
- Any admission in the allowance ends the hold, including other automatic work under the same root, such as a wake in a chat the agent started (claim_wake has no paused-status gate; the coordinator's pause is per conversation and chain). From then the rest of the held chat's wait counts, so a late /compact can still be refused as wall_budget. This is the safe choice because the other work's running time must count. ADR-134, both User Guide passages and test_other_automatic_work_in_the_allowance_ends_the_hold say so; TASK-33621.157 tracks starting the hold again once that work settles.
- The refusal path already keeps the stamp and the monotonic anchor through its rollback. It now also keeps `started_at`/`deadline_at` (only where the clock had already started), so a refused first retry neither loses the hold nor excludes it twice.
- Every path out of the hold: /compact, /rewind Summarize, Compact and send, and leaving Ask all lift the in-memory pause, and the retry's claim is an admission. App quit leaves the durable stamp: restart re-seeds the pause from the ledger, and the next admission ends it. By reading, Stop and Cancel all agents do not lift the hold (only `_lift_compaction_pauses` removes a coordinator pause), and neither does the user's own message (ADR-134 manual continuation). The hold then stays open until something admits the chain, and if nothing does, the clock never matters. settle() can replace the hold with tokens_budget (a late token overage) or review_required/usage_unknown without ending it. Both are final for the allowance, so the held time counting does not matter.
- Unchanged and still counting: queueing, tool approval waits (ADR-134; User Guide), the auto-off pause, every other pause code (pinned by test_other_pauses_keep_the_clock_running). Goal runs document that pauses, approvals and waits count; workflows already count only active intervals. Filed TASK-33621.154 for the owner to decide on approval waits and auto off.

**Lesson: a live "later wake held at Ask" needs the first wake delivered under Off, sizing from Next request minus overhead, and children that outlive their parent (2026-11-19).** This is the original branch's lessons-live-verification.md entry, moved here unchanged in substance.
- Incident: this task needed a chain whose first wake is accepted and whose second wake pauses at the Ask compaction trigger. Three live rounds failed before one worked.
  - A delivered wake's System notice ends the chat's compactable units (TASK-33621.155). So a chat that is over the trigger only after the first wake gives NON_COMPACTABLE, and nothing is held. The real-controller rig showed the same: 12 turns after a delivered wake never produced a hold (decision NON_COMPACTABLE at up to 3,358 of 1,800 tokens). The history that /compact can summarize has to come before the first wake. The round that worked sent one long turn, ran the first wake under `compaction_mode = "off"`, then set **When limit nears** to Ask in Chat settings while the second child ran.
  - A 1,500-token custom budget looked ample, but the second wake ran unheld. Chat settings ▸ Context and memory showed **Next request** 11,765 tokens and **Request overhead** 10,665 (tool schemas 8,874), so the history the trigger counts was only about 1,100 tokens. A 1,000-token budget (trigger 800) then held it at 1,067.
  - A child that settled 0.9 s before its parent's run row took its last update was refused as a non-survivor. The chain went to "Paused: work may have run" (TASK-33621.156).
- What to do: read **Next request** minus **Request overhead** before you pick a custom budget. Put the compactable history before the first wake, and run that wake under Off. Make each child outlive its parent's turn with the review proxy's `slow` mode and a multi-step child task. Read the chain and attempt rows read-only from `agent_runs.db` (`automatic_work_chains`, `automatic_wake_attempts`) after each step. Drive the round from a bash script that finds each click target by its text in `tmux capture-pane` output and sends SGR press and release sequences; under zsh, `set -- $XY` does not split the two coordinates, and the escape gets typed into whatever has focus.
